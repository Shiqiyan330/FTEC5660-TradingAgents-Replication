# Human-in-the-Loop Decisions

## When to ask the user

Request a human decision before proceeding when uncertainty or an error creates a consequential choice that could change the project's direction, research question, experimental scope, evaluation method, data source, model/provider, or interpretation of results. Also ask before taking an action with material cost, irreversible effects, or a substantial risk of invalidating prior work.

Examples include choosing between materially different experiment designs, replacing a data source after discovering a limitation, changing the model/provider after repeated failures, deciding whether to exclude failed runs or observations, and resolving ambiguous requirements that affect the validity of the project.

Do not interrupt for routine, low-risk, reversible troubleshooting. Gather evidence first, try a bounded diagnostic or a conservative fix within the user's stated scope, and report the result. If the same failure persists, the next attempt would materially change scope or assumptions, or the evidence does not support a clear choice, pause the affected work and ask.

## How to ask

Make the decision precise and easy to review. State:

1. What happened and what evidence is confirmed.
2. Which decision is blocked and why it matters.
3. The viable options and their practical trade-offs.
4. A recommendation, when evidence supports one.
5. One direct question asking the user to choose or approve the next direction.

Do not disguise a consequential choice as an implementation detail or silently choose a direction on the user's behalf. While waiting, continue independent work that does not depend on the decision. Clearly separate confirmed results from hypotheses and proposed next steps.
